Use QR codes for discovery

A practical way to discover a stage indoors or where GPS accuracy is insufficient.

When QR works well

QR labels fit museums, buildings, exhibitions and compact outdoor sites. A participant scans the label associated with a stage instead of depending only on their device’s location. You can use a QR label you already have or generate one from the stage editor.

Prepare a QR stage

  1. Create the road and stage. Write a clue that leads to the physical object or room without exposing the QR value itself.
  2. Enable discovery by QR in the stage form. Enter an existing code value or generate a code from the editor.
  3. Place or print the code where it can be scanned safely. Check lighting, reflections, print size and access for wheelchair users.
  4. Use a real phone to test the QR flow before the class starts. The activity page includes a scanner test when QR stages are present.

What students do

Open the game in a phone browser, allow camera access when asked and point the camera at the label. If the browser offers front and back cameras, choose the rear camera. The game records the successful discovery and presents the next available clue.

Camera permission is essential. If scanning does not open, check the browser’s site permission and the operating system’s camera permission. See the device troubleshooting guide.

Design considerations

Avoid putting answers or sensitive information inside a QR code. Treat the label as a discoverable marker: the meaningful learning content belongs in the stage clue and question. Keep a replacement printout for labels that are damaged or moved.
