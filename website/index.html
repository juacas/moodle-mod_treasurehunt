The adventure

From a course to a journey.

Treasure Hunt is a Moodle activity for location-based learning. Teachers design roads and stages; learners find places, solve questions and make progress on a real or virtual map.

◎

Explore

Use GPS outdoors, place a marker on the map from a desk, or scan QR codes where positioning is difficult.

◇

Think

Clues, optional questions and Moodle activity restrictions connect discovery with course content.

↗

Progress together

Play individually or in teams, choose grading rules and review progress in the teacher dashboard.

For teachers

Design the route. Let curiosity do the rest.

Create paths and stages in the geographic editor, draw areas or points, set discovery rules and preview each road before students play.

  • Choose individual or team play and configure groups.
  • Write clues for the next stage or the current stage.
  • Allow selected stages to be found out of sequence.
  • Import inspiration from public OpenData collections.

OpenData for authors

Turn nearby heritage into a stage.

The OpenData assistant searches georeferenced public records in the visible map area. Browse categories and providers, inspect descriptions, images and links, then add discoveries to a local layer. A useful place can become a stage with editable source information.

Search Wikidata and DBpedia; Europeana is available when its API key is configured.

The 3D horizon

Cessium brings the hunt to a globe.

Fly between places, explore terrain and follow an animated 3D Pegman. The Cessium / Cesium 3D viewer is an experimental alternative to the standard browser player. Mapbox is another optional experimental map viewer.
